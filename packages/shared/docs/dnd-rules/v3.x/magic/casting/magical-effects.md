# Combining Magical Effects

Spells or magical effects usually work as described, **no matter how many other spells or magical effects** happen to be operating in the same area or on the same recipient. Except in special cases, **a spell does not affect the way another spell operates**. 

Whenever a spell has a **specific effect on other spells**, the spell description explains that effect.

## General Rules

Several general rules apply when spells or magical effects operate in the same place:

## Stacking Effects

**Spells that provide bonuses or penalties** on attack rolls, damage rolls, saving throws, and other attributes usually **do not stack with themselves**. 

More generally, **two bonuses of the same type don't stack** even if they come from different spells (or from effects other than spells).

> **See**: [Bonus Types](#bonus-types) below

## Different Bonus Types

The bonuses or penalties from **two different spells stack** if the modifiers are of different types. A **bonus that isn't named** stacks with any bonus.

### Examples
- **Enhancement bonus** to Strength + **Morale bonus** to Strength = Both apply
- **Deflection bonus** to AC + **Armor bonus** to AC = Both apply  
- **Generic bonus** + **Named bonus** = Both apply

## Same Effect at Different Strengths

In cases when **two or more identical spells** are operating in the same area or on the same target, but at **different strengths**, only the **best one applies**.

### Example
- Character affected by **bull's strength** (+4 to Str) and **bull's strength** (+4 to Str) = Only one +4 applies
- Character affected by **bull's strength** (+4 to Str) and **bear's endurance** (+4 to Con) = Both apply (different abilities)

## Same Effect with Differing Results

The same spell can sometimes produce **varying effects** if applied to the same recipient more than once. Usually the **last spell in the series trumps the others**. 

**None of the previous spells are actually removed or dispelled**, but their effects become irrelevant while the final spell in the series lasts.

### Example
- **Polymorph** into wolf, then **polymorph** into bear = Character is a bear
- When bear form ends, wolf form does **not** resume (both spells ended)

## One Effect Makes Another Irrelevant

Sometimes, **one spell can render a later spell irrelevant**. Both spells are still active, but one has rendered the other useless in some fashion.

### Example
- Character under **hold person** (paralyzed) then affected by **haste** = Haste provides no benefit while paralyzed, but haste is still active

## Mental Control Effects

Sometimes magical effects that establish **mental control** render each other irrelevant, such as a spell that removes the subject's ability to act. 

**Mental controls that don't remove the recipient's ability to act** usually do not interfere with each other.

### Multiple Controllers
If a creature is under the mental control of **two or more creatures**, it tends to **obey each to the best of its ability**, and to the extent of the control each effect allows. 

If the controlled creature receives **conflicting orders simultaneously**, the competing controllers must make **opposed Charisma checks** to determine which one the creature obeys.

## Spells with Opposite Effects

Spells with **opposite effects** apply normally, with all bonuses, penalties, or changes accruing in the order that they apply. Some spells **negate or counter each other**. This is a special effect that is noted in a spell's description.

### Examples
- **Bless** (+1 morale bonus) and **bane** (-1 morale penalty) = Net effect +0
- **Haste** and **slow** = Usually one negates the other (check spell descriptions)

## Instantaneous Effects

**Two or more spells with instantaneous durations** work cumulatively when they affect the same target.

### Example
- **Cure light wounds** (1d8+1) + **Cure light wounds** (1d8+1) = Both healing amounts apply

## Bonus Types

Usually, a bonus has a **type** that indicates how the spell grants the bonus. The important aspect of bonus types is that **two bonuses of the same type don't generally stack**.

### Stacking Exceptions
With the exception of:
- **Dodge bonuses** (always stack)
- **Most circumstance bonuses** (usually stack if from different sources)
- **Racial bonuses** (stack with other types)

Only the **better bonus works**. The same principle applies to **penalties**—a character taking two or more penalties of the same type applies only the **worst one**.

### Common Bonus Types
- **Alchemical**: From alchemical substances
- **Armor**: From armor or natural armor
- **Circumstance**: From favorable circumstances
- **Competence**: From skill or knowledge
- **Deflection**: Protective force deflecting attacks
- **Dodge**: From agility and reflexes (always stacks)
- **Enhancement**: Magical improvement to existing bonus
- **Insight**: From divine guidance or revealed knowledge
- **Luck**: From good fortune
- **Morale**: From hope, courage, and determination
- **Profane**: From evil sources
- **Racial**: From creature's race or type
- **Resistance**: From magical protections
- **Sacred**: From good sources
- **Size**: From creature's size

## Special Spell Effects

Many special spell effects are handled according to the **school of the spells** in question. Certain other special spell features are found across spell schools.

### Attacks
Some spell descriptions refer to **attacking**. All offensive combat actions, even those that don't damage opponents, are considered attacks:
- **Attempts to turn or rebuke undead** count as attacks
- **All spells that opponents resist with saving throws** are attacks
- **Spells that deal damage** are attacks
- **Spells that otherwise harm or hamper subjects** are attacks
- **Spells that summon monsters or other allies** are **not** attacks (the spells themselves don't harm anyone)

### Bringing Back the Dead

Several spells have the power to **restore slain characters to life**.

When a living creature dies, its **soul departs its body**, leaves the Material Plane, travels through the Astral Plane, and goes to abide on the plane where the creature's deity resides. If the creature did not worship a deity, its soul departs to **the plane corresponding to its alignment**.

**Bringing someone back from the dead** means retrieving their soul and returning it to their body.

#### Level Loss
Any creature brought back to life usually **loses one level of experience**. The character's new XP total is **midway between the minimum needed** for their new (reduced) level and the minimum needed for the next one.

If the character was **1st level at the time of death**, they lose **2 points of Constitution** instead of losing a level.

#### Permanent Loss
This **level loss or Constitution loss cannot be repaired by any mortal means**, even **wish** or **miracle**. 

A revived character can **regain a lost level by earning XP** through further adventuring. A revived 1st-level character can regain lost Constitution by **improving their Constitution score** when they attain a level that allows an ability score increase.

#### Preventing Revivification
Enemies can take steps to make it more difficult for a character to be returned from the dead:
- **Keeping the body** prevents others from using **raise dead** or **resurrection**
- Casting **trap the soul** prevents any sort of revivification unless the soul is first released

#### Revivification Against One's Will
A **soul cannot be returned to life if it does not wish to be**. A soul knows the **name, alignment, and patron deity** (if any) of the character attempting to revive it and may refuse to return on that basis.
