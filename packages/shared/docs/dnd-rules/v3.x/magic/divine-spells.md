# Divine Spells

**Clerics, druids, experienced paladins, and experienced rangers** can cast divine spells. Unlike arcane spells, divine spells draw power from a **divine source**:

- **Clerics** — Gain spell power from deities or divine forces
- **Druids & Rangers** — Powered by the divine force of nature
- **Paladins** — Powered by divine forces of law and good

Divine spells tend to focus on **healing and protection** and are less flashy, destructive, and disruptive than arcane spells.

## Preparing Divine Spells

Divine spellcasters prepare their spells in largely the same manner as wizards do, but with a few differences. The **relevant ability for divine spells is Wisdom**.

### Requirements
- To prepare a divine spell, a character must have a **Wisdom score of 10 + the spell's level**
- **Bonus spells** are based on Wisdom

## Time of Day

A divine spellcaster chooses and prepares spells **ahead of time**, just as a wizard does. However, a divine spellcaster does **not require a period of rest** to prepare spells. 

Instead, the character chooses a **particular part of the day to pray and receive spells**. The time is usually associated with some daily event.

### Prayer Timing Rules
- If some event **prevents** a character from praying at the proper time, he must do so **as soon as possible**
- If the character does **not stop to pray** for spells at the first opportunity, he must **wait until the next day** to prepare spells

## Spell Selection and Preparation

A divine spellcaster selects and prepares spells ahead of time through **prayer and meditation** at a particular time of day.

### Preparation Requirements
- **Time required**: 1 hour (same as wizard)
- **Environment**: Relatively peaceful environment required
- **Fresh mind**: Only during first daily spell preparation

### Preparation Rules
- A divine spellcaster does **not have to prepare all spells at once**
- Cannot fill a slot that is empty because of casting or abandoning a spell (except during first preparation)
- **No spellbooks required** (unlike wizards)

### Spell Lists and Domains
- Spell selection is **limited to the spells on the list** for the character's class
- **Clerics, druids, paladins, and rangers** have separate spell lists
- **Clerics** also have access to **two domains** determined during character creation

#### Domain Spells (Clerics Only)
- Each domain gives access to a **domain spell at each spell level** from 1st to 9th
- Each domain provides a **special granted power**
- Must prepare **one domain spell per spell level** as an extra spell
- If a domain spell is **not on the cleric spell list**, it can only be prepared in a **domain spell slot**

## Spell Slots

The character class tables show **how many spells of each level** a character can cast per day. These openings for daily spells are called **spell slots**.

### Slot Rules
- A spellcaster can **fill a higher-level slot with a lower-level spell**
- A spellcaster who **lacks sufficient ability score** still gets the slots but must fill them with **lower-level spells**

## Recent Casting Limit

As with arcane spells, at the time of preparation any spells cast within the **previous 8 hours** count against the number of spells that can be prepared.

## Spontaneous Casting

### Cure and Inflict Spells (Clerics)
- **Good clerics** (or clerics of good deities) can spontaneously cast a **cure spell** in place of a prepared spell of the same level or higher (not domain spells)
- **Evil clerics** (or clerics of evil deities) can spontaneously cast an **inflict spell** in place of a prepared spell of the same level or higher (not domain spells)
- **Neutral clerics** of neutral deities choose either cure or inflict spells during character creation

The divine energy of the substituted spell is **converted** into the cure or inflict spell as if that spell had been prepared all along.

### Summon Nature's Ally (Druids)
A **druid** can spontaneously cast a **summon nature's ally spell** in place of a prepared spell of the same level or higher. The divine energy conversion works the same as cleric spontaneous casting.

## Divine Magical Writings

Divine spells can be **written down and deciphered** just as arcane spells can.

### Reading Divine Scrolls
- Any character with the **Spellcraft skill** can attempt to decipher divine magical writing and identify it
- Only characters who have the spell (in its **divine form**) on their **class spell list** can cast a divine spell from a scroll

## New Divine Spells

Divine spellcasters most frequently gain new spells in one of the following two ways:

### Spells Gained at a New Level
Characters who can cast divine spells undertake a **certain amount of study between adventures**. Each time such a character receives a **new level of divine spells**, they learn new spells from that level **automatically**.

### Independent Research
A divine spellcaster can **research a spell independently**, much as an arcane spellcaster can. Only the **creator** of such a spell can prepare and cast it, unless they decide to **share it with others**.
