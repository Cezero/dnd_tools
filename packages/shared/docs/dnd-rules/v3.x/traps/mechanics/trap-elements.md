# Trap Elements

All traps—mechanical or magical—have the following elements: **trigger**, **reset**, and **effect**. Some traps also include optional elements, such as **bypass**.

## Triggers

A trap's trigger determines how it is set off.

### Location Triggers
A **location trigger** goes off when someone enters the trigger area. Unless otherwise noted, location triggers are **5 feet square**.

### Proximity Triggers  
This trigger activates the trap when a creature approaches within a certain distance of it. A **proximity trigger** differs from a location trigger in that the creature need not be standing in a particular square. Creatures that are **flying** are just as likely to set it off as those that are walking. Proximity triggers are common only for magic traps.

**Proximity Trigger Range:**
- **Close range**: 25 ft. + 5 ft./2 levels
- **Medium range**: 100 ft. + 10 ft./level  
- **Long range**: 400 ft. + 40 ft./level

### Sound Triggers
This trigger springs a magic trap when it detects any sound. A sound trigger functions like an ear and has a **+15 bonus on Listen checks**. A successful **Move Silently check**, **magical silence**, and other effects that would negate hearing defeat it. A trap with a sound trigger requires the casting of **clairaudience** during its construction.

### Visual Triggers
This trigger for magic traps works like an actual eye, springing the trap whenever it "sees" something. A trap with a visual trigger requires the casting of **arcane eye**, **clairvoyance**, or **true seeing** during its construction. 

**Sight Range and Spot Bonus:**

| **Spell** | **Sight Range** | **Spot Bonus** |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------|
| arcane eye | Line of sight (unlimited range) | +20 |
| clairvoyance | One preselected location | +15 |
| true seeing | Line of sight (up to 120 ft.) | +30 |

**Special Considerations:**
- To "see" in the dark, choose **true seeing** or add **darkvision** (limits range to 60 ft.)
- **Invisibility**, **disguises**, or **illusions** that fool the base spell can fool the trigger

### Touch Triggers
A **touch trigger** springs the trap when touched and is one of the simplest kinds of trigger to construct. This trigger may be physically attached to the damage-dealing mechanism or separate. You can make a magic touch trigger by adding **alarm** to the trap and reducing the area of effect to cover only the trigger spot.

### Timed Triggers
This trigger periodically springs the trap after a certain duration has passed.

**Common Intervals:**
- **1 round** (immediate response)
- **1 minute** (short delay)
- **10 minutes** (exploration delay)
- **1 hour** (extended delay)
- **Custom timing** (set by builder)

### Spell Triggers
All **spell traps** have this kind of trigger. The appropriate spell descriptions explain the trigger conditions for traps that contain spell triggers.

## Reset Elements

A reset element determines the conditions under which a trap becomes ready to trigger again.

### No Reset
Short of completely rebuilding the trap, there's no way to trigger it more than once. **Spell traps** have no reset element.

### Repair Reset
To get the trap functioning again, you must **repair it**.

**Repair Requirements:**
- **Craft (trapmaking)** check against original building DC
- **Raw materials**: One-fifth of trap's original market price
- **Time**: Same calculation as building, using repair cost

### Manual Reset
Resetting the trap requires someone to **move the parts back into place**. This is the most common reset element for mechanical traps.

**Manual Reset Details:**
- **Time**: Usually 1 minute
- **Skill**: May require specific knowledge
- **Access**: Must reach trap mechanism safely

### Automatic Reset
The trap resets itself, either **immediately** or after a **timed interval**.

**Automatic Reset Types:**
- **Immediate**: Ready to trigger again next round
- **Delayed**: Resets after set time (1 minute to 1 hour)
- **Conditional**: Resets when specific condition is met

## Bypass Elements (Optional)

If the builder wants to move past the trap after creation, a **bypass mechanism** temporarily disarms it. Bypass elements are typically used only with **mechanical traps**; spell traps usually have built-in allowances for the caster.

### Lock Bypass
A **lock bypass** requires a **DC 30 Open Lock check** to open.

### Hidden Switch Bypass
A **hidden switch** requires a **DC 25 Search check** to locate.

### Hidden Lock Bypass  
A **hidden lock** combines both features:
- **DC 25 Search check** to locate
- **DC 30 Open Lock check** to open

### Custom Bypass
Builders can create unique bypass methods:
- **Password or phrase**
- **Specific item or key**
- **Magical condition** (detect thoughts, etc.)
- **Sequence of actions**
