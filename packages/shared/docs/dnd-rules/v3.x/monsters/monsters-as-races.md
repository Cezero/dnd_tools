# Monsters As Races

Many monster types can be used as player character races with appropriate level adjustments. This section provides guidance for using monsters as playable races.

## Available Monster Races

- **Centaur** — Half-human, half-horse with mounted combat advantages
- **Doppelganger** — Shapeshifting infiltrator with change self ability
- **Gnoll** — Hyena-like humanoid with natural weapons
- **Goblin** — Small humanoid with stealth and nimbleness
- **Grimlock** — Blind underground humanoid with enhanced senses

### Dwarf Variants

- **Dwarf, Psionic Duergar** — Enhanced duergar with additional mental powers

### Gnome Variants

## Suitability Guidelines

While every monster has the statistics that a player would need to play the creature as a character, **most monsters are not suitable as PCs**. 

**Unsuitable Creatures:**
- Creatures with **Intelligence score of 2 or lower**
- Creatures with **no way to communicate**
- Creatures **so different from other PCs** that they disrupt the campaign
- Creatures with **strange innate abilities or great physical power** (questionable except in high-level campaigns)

## Starting Level of a Monster PC

Monsters suitable for play have a **level adjustment** given in their statistics. Add a monster's level adjustment to its Hit Dice and Class Levels to get the creature's **effective character level (ECL)**. 

Effectively, monsters with a level adjustment become **multiclass characters** when they take class levels. A creature's **"monster class"** is always a favored class, and the creature never takes XP penalties for having it.

### Humanoids and Class Levels

**Creatures with 1 or less HD:**
- **Replace** their monster levels with their character levels
- **Lose** the attack bonus, saving throw bonuses, skills, and feats granted by 1 monster HD
- **Gain** the attack bonus, save bonuses, skills, feats, and other class abilities of a 1st-level character of the appropriate class

**Characters with more than 1 Hit Die:**
- Do **not** get a feat for their first class level (unlike common races)
- Do **not** multiply skill points for their first class level by four
- **Already received** a feat for their first Hit Die because of race
- **Already multiplied** their racial skill points for their first Hit Die by four

### Level Adjustment and Effective Character Level

To determine the **effective character level (ECL)** of a monster character, add its **level adjustment** to its **racial Hit Dice** and **character class levels**.

**ECL Usage:**
- Use **ECL instead of character level** to determine experience points needed for next level
- Use **ECL to determine starting wealth** for a monster character
- Monster characters treat **skills mentioned in their monster entry as class skills**

**Starting Requirements:**
- **1 Hit Die or less** OR **template creature**: Must start with **one or more class levels** (like regular character)
- **2 or more Hit Dice**: Can start with **no class levels** (though can gain them later)
- Even creatures that normally advance by Hit Dice can **gain class levels rather than Hit Dice**

### Hit Dice
The creature's **Hit Dice** equal the number of **class levels** it has plus its **racial Hit Dice**. Additional Hit Dice gained from taking levels in a character class **never affect a creature's size** like additional racial Hit Dice do.

### Feat Acquisition and Ability Score Increases
A monster's **total Hit Dice**, not its ECL, govern its acquisition of **feats and ability score increases**.

## Ability Scores for Monster PCs

While a monster's statistics give the ability scores for a **typical creature** of a certain kind, any "monster" creature that becomes an adventurer is **definitely not typical**. 

### Calculating Racial Modifiers

**For scores of 10 or higher:**
1. Check if the creature's entry has any ability scores of **10 or higher**
2. For each score, subtract:
   - **10** (if the score is even)
   - **11** (if the score is odd)
3. This gives the creature's **modifier for that ability** based on its race or kind
4. **Generate the character's ability scores** as normal
5. **Add the racial ability modifiers** to get final ability scores

> **Note**: Some monsters have base ability scores other than 10 and 11. If alternate scores were used this will be indicated in the monster entry. Also, some monsters that make good PCs have their **racial ability modifiers and other traits already listed** in their monster entry.

### For Ability Scores Lower Than 10

The procedure is different for low ability scores:

1. **Determine the character's ability scores** first
2. **Compare that number** to the monster's average ability score
3. Use either the **Intelligence table** or the **other abilities table** below

**Intelligence Table Purpose:**
The separate table for Intelligence ensures that **no PC ends up with an Intelligence score lower than 3**. This is important because:
- Creatures with **Intelligence lower than 3** are not playable characters
- Creatures with **any ability score lower than 1** are also not playable

## Other Statistics for Monsters

**Creatures with Hit Dice of 1 or less:**
- Have **normal, class-based Hit Dice** and features
- Get **skills and feats** appropriate to a 1st-level character (even if they have a level adjustment)

**Creatures with 2 or more Hit Dice:**
- Have statistics based on **racial Hit Dice** plus Hit Dice for **class levels** (if any)

## Experience for Monsters

**1 HD or less, no level adjustment:**
- Uses the **same tables as standard PC races** for experience needed

**1 HD or less, with level adjustment:**
- **Class levels + level adjustment** = experience requirement

**More than 1 HD, with level adjustment:**
- **Hit Dice + level adjustment + class level** = experience requirement
