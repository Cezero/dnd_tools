# Forest Fires (CR 6)

Forest fires represent one of the most dangerous natural hazards adventurers can encounter in wilderness areas.

## Fire Ignition

### Common Causes
- **Campfire sparks** in dry conditions
- **Lightning strikes** setting trees ablaze
- **Strong winds** spreading small fires
- **Dried forest floor** creating tinder conditions

### Conditions for Fire
Most campfire sparks ignite nothing, but fires can result when:
- **Conditions are dry**
- **Winds are strong**  
- **Forest floor is dried out and flammable**

## Detection and Approach

### Spotting Forest Fires
- **Detection distance**: 2d6 × 100 feet
- **Spot check**: Treat fire as Colossal creature (DC reduced by 16)
- **Failed checks**: Fire moves closer to characters
- **Automatic detection**: When fire closes to half original distance

### Heat Detection
- **Blinded characters** or those unable to make Spot checks
- **Heat sensation**: Automatically "spot" fire when **100 feet away**
- **No check required** for heat detection

## Fire Mechanics

### Fire Movement
- **Leading edge speed**: 120 feet per round (moderate winds)
- **Advancement**: Faster than human running speed
- **Burn duration**: 2d4 × 10 minutes before dying to smoldering
- **Trapping effect**: Characters may be trapped deeper as fire advances

### Fire Direction
- **Leading edge**: Downwind side advances fastest
- **Wind direction**: Determines fire movement pattern
- **Escape routes**: Must consider wind patterns

## Dangers Within Fire

Characters within forest fire bounds face **three primary dangers**:

### 1. Heat Damage
**Worse than extreme heat exposure**:

#### Breathing Damage
- **Automatic damage**: 1d6 points per round (no save)
- **Fortitude saves**: Every 5 rounds (DC 15, +1 per previous check)
- **Nonlethal damage**: 1d4 points on failed save

#### Heat Protection
- **Holding breath**: Avoids lethal damage, not nonlethal
- **Clothing penalty**: Heavy clothing or armor imposes -4 penalty on saves
- **Metal effects**: Metal armor affected as if by *heat metal* spell

### 2. Catching on Fire
**Fire exposure risks**:

#### Initial Risk
- **Leading edge**: Risk when fire overtakes characters
- **Ongoing risk**: Once per minute thereafter
- **See standard catching fire rules** for mechanics

#### Fire Effects
- **Damage**: As per catching fire rules
- **Spreading**: Fire can spread to equipment
- **Extinguishing**: Requires appropriate actions

### 3. Smoke Inhalation
**Heavy smoke effects**:

#### Smoke Saves
- **Fortitude save**: Each round (DC 15, +1 per previous check)
- **Failure**: Spend round choking and coughing
- **Consecutive choking**: 2 rounds = 1d6 nonlethal damage

#### Vision Effects
- **Concealment**: Smoke obscures vision for all within it
- **Miss chance**: Standard concealment penalties apply
- **Navigation**: Severely impaired by smoke

## Survival Strategies

### Early Detection
- **Maintain Spot checks** in dry conditions
- **Watch for smoke** on horizon
- **Listen for crackling** sounds
- **Smell for smoke** scents

### Escape Tactics
- **Determine wind direction** to predict fire movement
- **Move perpendicular** to fire advancement when possible
- **Seek firebreaks**: Rivers, clearings, rocky areas
- **Avoid upwind movement** from fire

### Protection Methods
- **Wet clothing** if water available
- **Cover nose/mouth** to reduce smoke inhalation
- **Stay low** where smoke is thinner
- **Remove metal armor** to avoid heat metal effects

### Emergency Shelters
- **Rocky outcroppings** may provide protection
- **Water bodies** can serve as refuge
- **Previously burned areas** less likely to burn again
- **Underground locations** if available

## Environmental Conditions

### Wind Effects
- **Fire spread**: Strong winds accelerate fire advancement
- **Direction changes**: Wind shifts can redirect fire
- **Spotting**: Winds can carry burning debris ahead of main fire

### Terrain Factors
- **Uphill fire**: Travels faster uphill than downhill
- **Fuel load**: Dense undergrowth accelerates fire
- **Moisture**: Recent rain reduces fire risk
- **Barriers**: Rivers and clearings can slow fire

### Weather Influence
- **Dry conditions**: Increase fire risk and speed
- **Humidity**: Low humidity accelerates burning
- **Recent precipitation**: Reduces fire likelihood
- **Temperature**: High heat increases fire intensity

## Aftermath Effects

### Burned Areas
- **No undergrowth** for extended periods
- **Fallen trees** create difficult terrain
- **Ash layers** may hide hazards
- **Reduced animal life** in immediate aftermath

### Long-term Changes
- **Regrowth patterns** over seasons
- **New clearings** where dense forest burned
- **Changed water flow** due to reduced vegetation
- **Altered wildlife** patterns and populations
