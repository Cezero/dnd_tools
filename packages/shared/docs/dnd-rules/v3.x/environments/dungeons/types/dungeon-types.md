# Dungeon Types

The four basic dungeon types are defined by their **current status**. Many dungeons are variations on these basic types or combinations of more than one of them. Sometimes old dungeons are used again and again by different inhabitants for different purposes.

## Ruined Structure

**Once occupied**, this place is now **abandoned** (completely or in part) by its original creator or creators, and **other creatures have wandered in**.

### Characteristics
- **Original purpose**: Previously had intelligent occupants and specific function
- **Current state**: Abandoned by original inhabitants
- **New inhabitants**: Subterranean creatures seeking shelter
- **Trap status**: Most traps probably **set off already**
- **Wandering creatures**: **Very common** - various beasts and monsters

### Typical Features
- **Deteriorating architecture** from lack of maintenance
- **Overgrown areas** where nature has reclaimed spaces
- **Partially collapsed** sections creating new passages
- **Evidence of past occupation** (furniture, decorations, tools)
- **Mixed creature lairs** in different sections

### Exploration Considerations
- **Lower trap danger** but not eliminated entirely
- **Higher encounter rate** with wandering monsters
- **Unpredictable layouts** due to structural changes
- **Potential treasure** left behind by original inhabitants
- **Multiple creature territories** may overlap

## Occupied Structure

This type of dungeon is **still in use**. Creatures (usually intelligent) live there, although they may not be the dungeon's creators.

### Current Functions
An occupied structure might serve as:
- **Home** for intelligent creatures
- **Fortress** for military purposes
- **Temple** for religious activities
- **Active mine** for resource extraction
- **Prison** for containing captives
- **Headquarters** for organizations

### Characteristics
- **Active inhabitants**: Usually intelligent creatures
- **Maintained condition**: Kept in working order
- **Organized security**: Guards on watch and patrol
- **Controlled hazards**: Traps and creatures under inhabitant control
- **Functional layout**: Designed for current occupants' needs

### Security Features
- **Guard rotations** with established patterns
- **Communication systems** between areas
- **Controlled access** to the outside
- **Deliberate trap placement** in strategic locations
- **Trained creature guardians** under command

### Infrastructure
- **Appropriate furnishings** for inhabitants
- **Decorations** reflecting current culture
- **Supply storage** for ongoing needs
- **Suitable passages** for occupants to move through
- **Functional doors** that inhabitants can operate

### Mixed Occupation
Some dungeons are **partially occupied and partially empty** or in ruins. In such cases:
- **Occupants are typically not the original builders**
- **Intelligent creatures** have established a base within an abandoned dungeon
- **Clear boundaries** between occupied and abandoned sections
- **Different security levels** in different areas

## Safe Storage

When people want to **protect something**, they might bury it underground. Whether the item is a **fabulous treasure**, **forbidden artifact**, or **important figure's remains**, these valuable objects are placed within a dungeon surrounded by barriers, traps, and guardians.

### Purpose and Design
- **Protection of valuables**: Primary function is security
- **Function over form**: Built for effectiveness rather than appearance
- **Specialized construction**: Designed to prevent intrusion
- **Long-term storage**: Built to last without maintenance

### Security Measures
- **Most likely to have traps** of all dungeon types
- **Least likely to have wandering beasts** (controlled environment)
- **Deliberate guardian placement**: Creatures specifically assigned to protect
- **Multiple layers of defense**: Redundant security systems

### Guardian Types

#### Living Guardians
- **Magical sustenance** required to keep creatures alive
- **Food and water provision** through magical means
- **Rotation schedules** for living guards
- **Communication with outside** for support

#### Undead Guardians
- **No sustenance requirements**: Don't need food, water, or rest
- **Permanent vigilance**: Never sleep or become distracted
- **Immunity to many effects**: Difficult to bypass or eliminate
- **Thematic appropriateness**: Especially common in tombs

#### Construct Guardians
- **No biological needs**: Require no food, water, air, or rest
- **Single-purpose design**: Built specifically for guardian duties
- **Magical activation**: Can remain dormant until needed
- **Durable construction**: Resistant to wear and time

#### Summoned Guardians
- **Magic trap activation**: Appear only when needed
- **No sustenance required**: Disappear when task is complete
- **Fresh and alert**: Always at full strength when summoned
- **Variety of options**: Different creatures for different threats

### Ornamentation
- **Functional decoration**: Statuary and painted walls in important areas
- **Tomb decoration**: Particularly elaborate for important figures
- **Cultural elements**: Reflecting the builders' traditions
- **Warning symbols**: Deterrent messages and threats

## Natural Cavern Complex

Underground caves provide homes for all sorts of **subterranean monsters**. Created naturally and connected by a **labyrinthine tunnel system**, these caverns lack any sort of **pattern, order, or decoration**.

### Natural Characteristics
- **No intelligent design**: Formed by natural geological processes
- **Irregular layout**: No predictable pattern or organization
- **Connected systems**: Labyrinthine tunnels link multiple caverns
- **Varied chamber sizes**: From tight passages to vast underground halls
- **Natural formations**: Stalactites, stalagmites, underground streams

### Security Absence
- **Least likely to have traps**: No intelligent builder to place them
- **Few or no doors**: Natural openings and passages
- **No deliberate defenses**: Only natural barriers and hazards
- **Random encounters**: Creatures following natural behavior patterns

### Ecosystem Features

#### Fungal Forests
- **Thriving fungi**: All sorts flourish in cave environments
- **Huge mushroom forests**: Sometimes growing to enormous sizes
- **Puffball colonies**: Various species and sizes
- **Food chain base**: Supporting entire ecosystems

#### Natural Lighting
- **Phosphorescent fungi**: Provide limited natural light sources
- **Magical enhancement**: *Daylight* spells enable green plant growth
- **Varying illumination**: Some areas lit, others in complete darkness
- **Ecological zones**: Different lighting supports different life

#### Predator Activity
- **Subterranean hunters**: Prowl fungal forests for prey
- **Natural hunting grounds**: Well-established territories
- **Prey relationships**: Creatures feeding on fungi attract predators
- **Migration patterns**: Seasonal or resource-driven movement

### Connections to Other Dungeons
- **Discovery during construction**: Manufactured dungeons may break into natural caves
- **Connecting passages**: Link otherwise unrelated dungeon complexes
- **Mixed environments**: Create strange combinations of natural and artificial
- **Creature migration routes**: How subterranean monsters populate manufactured dungeons
- **Expanded complexes**: Natural caves can greatly increase dungeon size

### Exploration Challenges
- **Easy to get lost**: No logical layout or landmarks
- **Unpredictable encounters**: Natural creature behavior
- **Environmental hazards**: Unstable formations, underground rivers
- **Navigation difficulty**: Multiple levels and confusing passages

---

## Combination and Variation Types

### Sequential Occupation
- **Multiple builders**: Different groups over time
- **Layered construction**: New additions to existing structures
- **Cultural mixing**: Architecture and decoration from different periods
- **Adapted layouts**: Modifications for new purposes

### Partial Combinations
- **Mixed security**: Some areas trapped, others naturally defended
- **Varied occupancy**: Active, ruined, and storage areas in same complex
- **Different builders**: Natural caves expanded with constructed areas
- **Evolutionary development**: Dungeons changing over time
