# Dungeons

Comprehensive guide to underground environments, from natural caves to constructed fortresses.

## Core Documentation

### [Dungeon Types](types/dungeon-types.md)
Understanding the four basic categories of underground complexes:
- **Ruined Structure** — Abandoned places now inhabited by wandering creatures
- **Occupied Structure** — Active locations with intelligent inhabitants
- **Safe Storage** — Vaults and tombs designed to protect valuables
- **Natural Cavern Complex** — Underground cave systems formed naturally

### [Walls and Floors](terrain/walls-floors.md)
Complete reference for dungeon terrain elements:
- **Wall types** — Masonry, hewn stone, iron, magical treatments
- **Floor varieties** — Flagstone, rubble, smooth stone, special features
- **Structural statistics** — Break DCs, hardness, hit points, climb DCs

### Common Floor Types
- **Flagstone** — Most common, fitted stones with cracks
- **Hewn stone** — Carved from rock, rough but durable
- **Natural stone** — Cave floors with irregular surfaces
- **Rubble** — Debris areas affecting movement

## Dungeon Design Principles

### Security Considerations
- **Trap placement** varies significantly by dungeon type
- **Guardian strategies** depend on intended purpose
- **Access control** through doors, barriers, and chokepoints
- **Communication systems** in occupied structures

### Structural Elements
- **Wall thickness** determines structural integrity
- **Material choice** affects durability and cost
- **Special features** like arrow slits provide tactical advantages
- **Magical enhancement** can improve all properties

### Environmental Factors
- **Moisture** common in natural and poorly maintained areas
- **Fungal growth** in damp, dark environments
- **Air circulation** through natural or constructed ventilation
- **Lighting** from artificial sources or phosphorescent fungi

## Exploration Guidelines

### Safety Protocols
- **Test floor stability** before putting full weight
- **Check wall integrity** before climbing or leaning
- **Listen for sounds** through walls and floors
- **Search for hidden** passages and features

### Navigation Techniques
- **Map thoroughly** to avoid getting lost
- **Mark safe areas** and dangerous zones
- **Identify patterns** in construction or layout
- **Note air currents** that may indicate passages

### Resource Management
- **Light sources** essential in most areas
- **Climbing equipment** for scaling walls
- **Tools for breaking** through barriers when needed
- **Detection equipment** for finding traps and secrets
